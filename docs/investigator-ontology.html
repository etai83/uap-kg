How a testimony becomes a theory of the case

This page gives the flow from raw data to a theory of the case. Read the diagram first. Then read each term in the same order. You are the curator. Only you approve an entity mention. Only you set a claim to live. Only you verify a finding or a theory.

Terms for the scope

Scope
Case

A case is one investigation.

A case is not an event.

A case holds one or more events.

You attach each finding and each theory to one case.

Lazar/S4 is a case. Nimitz is a case.

Scope
Event

An event is one incident in the world.

An event is not a video.

Persons can refer to the same event in many videos.

If persons do not agree about one event, you keep one event.

Approved persons, locations, and dates can be keys for an event.

Those entities do not create the event by themselves.

Scope
Video

A video is one media file from a channel.

A video is not an event.

A video can hold one or more tellings.

Scope
Channel

A channel is the YouTube channel that published the video.

The same claimant on a different channel does not make a new support.

Scope
Person

A person is one real individual.

A person is not a speaker label.

The same person can be the claimant when that person spoke.

The same person can be only a name in the text.

You use claimants when you test independence.

Step 2
Speaker label

A speaker label marks one voice in one video.

A speaker label is not a person.

You map the label to the claimant.

A live claim cannot keep only the speaker label.

Scope
Telling

A telling is one account of one event in one video.

The telling shows the persons who spoke.

A telling is not an event.

A telling is not a claim.

One telling does not become a finding.

Scope
Facet

A facet is the aspect of one point.

Time, place, and the object are facets.

A facet is not an entity.

A facet is not an event.

A finding can name one facet.

Terms for the testimony

Step 1
Raw

Raw data is the capture of one source.

Raw data shows the video identity and the time marks.

Raw data does not show that an assertion is true.

You start the flow with raw data.

Step 2
Processed span

A processed span is text that you make from raw data.

The span shows the speaker label and the time in the video.

The span does not show that an assertion is true.

You extract entity mentions from the span.

You also make claims from the span.

Step 2 to steps 3 and 4
Extraction

An extraction pulls structure from a processed span.

One extraction can make entity mentions.

One extraction can make claims.

You approve each extraction.

A claim is not live before that approval.

An entity mention is not approved before that approval.

Step 4
Assertion

An assertion is what the claimant stated.

A claim records that assertion.

An assertion is not a point that you accept.

Step 4
Atomic claim

An atomic claim records one assertion by one claimant.

The claimant is the person who made the claim.

You can approve an atomic claim by itself.

You split a claim that holds two assertions before it is live.

Step 4
Claim

A claim records that the claimant made one assertion.

A claim is an epistemic layer.

A claim is not a finding.

You set a claim to live when you approve the extraction.

A live claim names the claimant.

A live claim does not mean that the assertion is true.

A live claim can also name the other entities.

Step 4
Claimant

The claimant is the person who made the claim.

One atomic claim has one claimant.

You take the claimant from the speaker label on the span.

You map that label to a person.

A live claim must name the claimant.

The claimant is not a person who is only named in the text.

Type of a testimony node
Epistemic layer

An epistemic layer is the type of a testimony node.

The five layers are raw, processed, claim, inference, and assumption.

You set the layer when you create the node.

You do not change the layer later.

A case, an event, an entity, a finding, and a theory are not layers.

Terms for entities

Step 3
Entity

An entity is one durable thing outside the five layers.

An entity is not a claim.

An entity is not an event.

Two mentions can refer to one entity.

You approve that link.

You do not merge the mentions in silence.

Step 3
Entity mention

An entity mention is one reference in one processed span.

The mention shows the words and the time in the video.

A mention is proposed until you approve it.

Approval does not make an assertion true.

A claim can use an approved mention.

Step 3
Named person

A named person is a person the span talks about.

You record that person as an entity mention.

That role does not make the person the claimant.

The person is the claimant only when that person spoke the span.

Step 3
Object

An object is a physical thing named in a span.

An object is not a person.

An object is not a location.

A document or a vehicle can be an object.

An object is not a claim.

Step 3. Kind of object
UAP item

A UAP item is an object of UAP interest.

A craft can be a UAP item.

A material sample can be a UAP item.

A sensor track can be a UAP item.

A UAP item is not proof that the item exists.

A UAP item is not a claim.

Step 3
Location

A location is a place named in a span.

A location in the text is not the place of the video.

A location is not an event.

S-4 can be a location.

A ship position can be a location.

Step 3
Date and time

A date is a calendar time named in a span.

The span can also name a time of day.

This date is not the time mark of the span.

The time mark shows when the claimant spoke.

The named date shows when the claimant places the incident.

A date does not create an event by itself.

Step 3
Organization

An organization is a group named in a span.

A unit, an agency, or a company can be an organization.

An organization is not a person.

An organization is not an event.

Step 3
Entity type

The first entity types are named person, object, UAP item, location, date and time, and organization.

You can add another entity type.

A new type is not a new epistemic layer.

Terms beside the main path

Not a finding
Inference

An inference is a conclusion that the source does not state.

You make an inference from one or more claims.

An inference is not a claim.

An inference is not a finding.

Connects points in a theory
Assumption

An assumption is a premise that you adopt.

The source does not state the assumption.

You can use an assumption only to connect points in a theory.

You must identify each assumption.

An assumption is not a finding.

Terms for the comparison

Step 5
Comparison

A comparison sets the live claims of one case next to each other.

A comparison can make same-assertion links.

A comparison can make contradiction records, gaps, and open questions.

A comparison does not make a finding.

Step 5
Same-assertion link

A same-assertion link joins two claims.

The two claims make the same assertion.

The link keeps both claims.

The link does not make a finding.

Step 5
Contradiction record

A contradiction record holds a conflict between live claims.

The claims are about one point.

The record does not pick a winner.

You attach the record to a finding for that point.

Step 5
Gap

A gap is a point that no live claim covers.

A gap is not a contradiction.

You do not invent a claim to fill a gap.

You can record a gap as an open question.

Step 5
Open question

An open question records a point that is not settled.

An open question is not a claim.

Only you can open the question.

Only you can resolve the question.

Beside the five layers
Research object

A research object is a record beside the five layers.

A contradiction record is a research object.

An open question is a research object.

A finding is a research object.

A theory is a research object.

You keep these objects with the testimony.

Terms for the result

Gate before step 6
Independent

Two supports are independent only when two different claimants make the assertion.

The same claimant on two channels is not a second support.

That repeat is not independence.

Two claimants who only repeat one source are not independent.

You keep a record of that source.

Step 6
Finding

A finding records one point that you accept.

The finding shows the claims that support the point.

The finding can show the event and the facet.

You verify a finding only when the supports are independent.

You keep each conflict for that point on the finding.

When you verify a finding, you do not change the claims.

A finding is not a claim.

A finding is not an inference.

A finding is not a score.

Step 7
Theory

A theory is one account of one case.

Verified findings give the primary points of the theory.

You can use an assumption only to connect those points.

You must identify each assumption.

You sign the theory as its own act.

If you reject the theory, you can keep the findings.

One case can have more than one theory.

Terms for status

Status
Proposed

A proposed object is a draft.

You did not approve it.

You do not use a proposed object as support.

Status of a claim
Live

Live is a status of a claim.

You set this status when you approve the extraction.

Live does not mean that the assertion is true.

Status of a finding or a theory
Verified

Verified is a status of a finding or a theory.

You set this status when you sign the object.

A verified finding meets the support rules.

A verified theory is an account that you accept.

Status
Rejected

Rejected means that you declined the object.

You keep the rejected object.

You do not use it as support.

Status
Superseded

Superseded means that a newer object replaces this object.

You keep the old object.

You do not use it as support.

Which status you use

Object States Effect
Claim proposed · live · rejected · superseded You approve the extraction. You do not accept the assertion as true.
Finding proposed · verified · rejected · superseded Two different claimants make the assertion. You keep the conflicts for that point. You sign the finding.
Theory proposed · verified · rejected · superseded You sign the account. If you reject the theory, you can keep the verified findings.
